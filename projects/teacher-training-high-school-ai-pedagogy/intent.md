# Intent: High School Teacher AI Literacy & Pedagogy Training Framework

## 1. Problem / Opportunity Statement
High school students (Grades 9–12) have ubiquitous access to generative AI tools on their personal smartphones and laptops. In the absence of teacher guidance, the predominant student use-case is **passive cognitive offloading**: copy-pasting homework prompts into chatbots, copying the generated answers, and submitting them for marks. This creates severe **cognitive deskilling ("brain rot")**, illusion of competence, and a breakdown of analytical perseverance.

Simultaneously, high school teachers feel unprepared, anxious, or reactive, relying on flawed AI text detectors that damage trust. Teachers need hands-on AI literacy so they can:
1. **Redesign Pedagogy & Assessments**: Structure assignments that cannot be solved by passive copy-pasting and require visible metacognition.
2. **Harness AI for Personalized Socratic Coaching**: Teach students how to interact with AI as an intellectual sparring partner and personal coach, allowing every student to take on ambitious, real-world intellectual challenges.

## 2. Target Audience & Stakeholders
- **Primary**: Secondary & Senior Secondary Educators (Grades 9–12) teaching Science, Math, Social Studies, and Languages.
- **Secondary**: School Principals, Curriculum Directors, CBSE Academic Coordinators, and Parent-Teacher Associations.

## 3. Definition of "Done"
A classroom-tested, 4-week modular training curriculum and action guide containing:
- **Module 1**: Demystifying AI & The Cognitive Science of "Brain Rot" vs. Deep Learning.
- **Module 2**: Redesigning Homework & Assessments (Process-over-Product & In-Class Defenses).
- **Module 3**: The Socratic AI Coach (System Prompts for Personalized Inquiry & Challenge Escalation).
- **Module 4**: Ethics, Plagiarism Redefined, and Equipping Students for an AI-Native Future.
- Complete with ready-to-copy classroom prompt templates, rubrics, and diagnostic roleplays.

## 4. Constraints & Non-Negotiables
- Requires zero coding prerequisites for teachers.
- Directly addresses both high-resource (1:1 laptop) and low-resource (single smartphone/shared lab) Indian schools.
- Grounded in empirical educational literature and NEP 2020 guidelines.
