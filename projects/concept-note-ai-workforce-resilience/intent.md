# Intent: Indian IT/BPO Workforce Resilience & Centaur Transition

## 1. Problem / Opportunity Statement
India's IT-BPM sector employs over 5.4 million professionals and contributes ~7.5% of GDP. Historically, the business model relied on labor arbitrage and the billable-hour pyramid—staffed heavily by junior software engineers and BPO associates performing structured, repetitive tasks. With the emergence of reasoning LLMs capable of code generation, test automation, and multimodal conversational customer support, the billable-hour model is fracturing.

If India relies solely on natural attrition, millions of junior engineers face career stagnation and hiring freezes. However, if IT services firms proactively transition into "Centaur Solutions" (human expertise amplified by AI agent swarms), Indian firms can climb up the global value chain.

## 2. Target Audience & Stakeholders
- Executive leadership across Indian IT services firms and GCCs (Global Capability Centers).
- Ministry of Electronics and IT (MeitY) and Ministry of Skill Development.
- NASSCOM and national engineering universities.

## 3. Definition of "Done"
A 5-part strategic concept note providing:
1. Structural diagnosis of the billing-hour collapse.
2. The Centaur workforce blueprint.
3. Sectoral diversification into domestic AI implementation.
4. Tripartite reskilling policy framework (Government, Industry, Academia).

## 4. Constraints & Non-Negotiables
- Must acknowledge both threat vectors and new job creation frontiers.
- Empirical grounding using verified industry statistics.
