## Detailed Comparative Analysis

### 1. Strategy & Philosophy

**India** operates a "DPI-first, regulation-light, inclusion-oriented" model ([[source-indiaai-mission-progress-2024-2026]]). The guiding principle is that AI should be democratized through public infrastructure and extended to populations traditionally excluded from digital systems (490 million informal workers via ShramSetu) ([[source-niti-aayog-ai-inclusive-development-2025]]).

**China** follows a state-directed model where AI strategy flows from Party-State planning — five-year plans, municipal compute subsidies, and coordinated research consortia ([[meeting-13-ai-with-chinese-characteristics]]). The primary targets are industrial automation, manufacturing robotics, and physical AI integration.

**The US** relies on private sector innovation at unprecedented scale. American AI investment is concentrated in a handful of hyperscalers and frontier labs (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta AI) with government playing a facilitating rather than directing role.

**The EU** has positioned itself as the global regulatory standard-setter through the **EU AI Act** — the world's first binding comprehensive AI legislation.

### 2. Compute & Infrastructure

The compute gap between India and global leaders is the most striking disparity ([[india-vs-china-ai-strategy]]):

| Country/Region | Estimated AI Compute (GPUs or equivalent) | Model |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| India | 38,000+ | Government-subsidized marketplace |
| China | Hundreds of thousands | State-owned grids + domestic silicon |
| US | Millions | Private hyperscaler investment |
| EU | Tens of thousands | National compute initiatives |

India's marketplace model trades scale for capital efficiency — leveraging private providers with demand-side subsidies rather than direct state ownership.

### 3. Governance Spectrum

The global AI governance landscape in 2026 forms a spectrum from binding to permissive ([[source-india-ai-governance-guidelines-2025]]):

```
[Most Binding]                                                [Most Permissive]
   EU AI Act  →  China (CAC registration)  →  India (Seven Sutras)  →  US (NIST guidelines)
```

India occupies a unique position: more structured than the US (with institutional bodies like AIGG, TPEC, AISI) but more permissive than the EU (voluntary principles vs. binding classification).

## Strategic Implications for India

1. **Compute Gap as Defining Constraint**: India's 38,000 GPUs vs. China's hundreds of thousands highlights the fundamental resource asymmetry
2. **Application Layer as Competitive Advantage**: India's linguistic and demographic complexity creates a natural moat for contextualized AI deployment
3. **Governance Positioning as Strategic Asset**: The "Seven Sutras" framework could attract AI companies seeking innovation-friendly environments
4. **Multi-Alignment Risk**: Maintaining relationships across US and Chinese tech ecosystems may become untenable under intensifying geopolitical pressure
