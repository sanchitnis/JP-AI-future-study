# Source: AI in Science - Early Insights (2026)

**Title**: AI in Science: Early Insights
**Authors**: Mihir Codreanu, Alex Imas, Juan Mateos-Garcia, et al. (Google, Google DeepMind, MIT FutureTech, etc.)
**Date**: September 2026
**Source Link**: [`raw/literature/AI-in-Science.pdf`](../../../raw/literature/AI-in-Science.pdf)

## Executive Summary
This paper inaugurates a comprehensive evaluation of AI's impact on scientific research by analyzing 15 million Gemini interactions, an inventory of over 2,600 specialized AI models, and a survey of >600 scientists. The study finds that AI adoption in science outpaces the general workforce. LLMs and specialized models act as economic complements rather than substitutes. While researchers report massive productivity gains (saving ~7 hours per week), these time savings are uncovering downstream physical bottlenecks, leading to a growing backlog of untested hypotheses and pushing some researchers toward "safer," incremental science due to a high "verification tax."

## Atomic Grounded Claims & Data Points

### 1. Broad Adoption and Coverage
- **Over-representation**: Scientists use AI 1.8x to 2.7x more than the general workforce baseline. In core STEM fields, it reaches up to 5.8x.
- **Daily Usage**: 47% of surveyed researchers use AI daily; 31% use it weekly.
- **Usage Split**: Time is split roughly 70% on general LLMs (coding assistants, document analysis, writing) and 30% on specialized models (e.g., AlphaFold).

### 2. Complementarity of LLMs and Specialized Models
- **Division of Labor**: LLMs are primarily used for broad tasks such as quantitative data analysis, coding, and manuscript preparation. Specialized AI models are used for generation, prediction (e.g., protein folding), and complex domain simulation.
- **Task Overlap**: The correlation in usage across tasks falls monotonically as granularity increases. At the finest level, there is almost no overlap, indicating LLMs and specialized models function as economic complements.

### 3. Productivity Gains & The Verification Tax
- **Time Saved**: Researchers report an average net time saving of **6.9 hours per week**.
- **Reinvestment**: 84% of researchers report a net increase in lab/professional outputs over the last three years due to this time reinvestment.
- **The Verification Tax**: AI outputs are not "turn-key." Almost 90% of scientists who save time spend a meaningful share of it verifying AI outputs; 46% spend more than a quarter of their saved time auditing, debugging, or fact-checking.

### 4. Downstream Shift in Bottlenecks
- **Physical Bottlenecks**: As hypothesis generation and coding accelerate, the primary bottleneck has shifted downstream to physical wet-lab work, clinical validation, and field data collection.
- **Untested Backlogs**: 41% of scientists report a growing backlog of untested hypotheses.

### 5. The "Streetlight Effect" (Impact on Human Agency & Ambition)
- **Incremental Focus**: 49% of surveyed scientists report that AI pushes them toward "safer, more incremental questions" where data and benchmarks are established, compared to only 28% who say it encourages riskier questions.
- **Risk**: While AI lowers the cost of incremental work and boosts output volume, it risks crowding out high-risk, high-reward discoveries due to the high cost of physical validation.

## Implications for India & Study Group Perspectives

- **Compute & Hardware Sovereignty**: The paper notes that specialized model development is concentrated in the US, China, UK, and EU. This reinforces the arguments in [[india-semiconductor-and-hardware-sovereignty]] and the [[indiaai-mission]] that state-backed compute infrastructure is critical to avoiding a dependency on foreign scientific models.
- **Cognitive Offloading & Human Agency**: The high "verification tax" and the "Streetlight Effect" (pushing researchers toward safer questions) directly relate to the group's concerns in [[ai-cognitive-revolution-and-human-agency]] and [[genai-creativity-and-knowledge-work-economics]]. If AI simply increases the volume of low-quality, incremental papers, the strategic advantage shifts to those who can execute physical validation and riskier hypotheses.
- **Interdisciplinarity**: 68% of scientists reported that AI increased their access to insights from other disciplines, which supports the cross-pollination intent of the JP AI Future Study Group.
