# India AI Governance Guidelines 2025 — Seven Sutras Framework

**Source Type**: Government Policy Framework & Regulatory Analysis
**Publishers**: MeitY, Principal Scientific Adviser (PSA), PIB, White & Case LLP analysis
**Date Range**: November 2025 – September 2026
**Primary URLs**: pib.gov.in, psa.gov.in, meity.gov.in, whitecase.com, livelaw.in

---

## Overview

India released the **India AI Governance Guidelines** in November 2025, establishing a "light-touch," risk-based, principle-driven governance framework. Unlike the EU AI Act's binding regulatory approach, India chose a voluntarist "Seven Sutras" framework with institutional mechanisms designed to evolve from voluntary compliance toward mandatory standards.

---

## The Seven Guiding Sutras

1. **Trust as the Foundation** — AI systems must be designed and deployed to foster public trust and confidence.
2. **People-First** — AI must prioritize human dignity, rights, and welfare above all else.
3. **Innovation over Restraint** — Regulatory frameworks must enable, not hinder, responsible innovation.
4. **Fairness & Equity** — AI must address and mitigate societal biases, especially in high-stakes applications.
5. **Safety, Resilience & Sustainability** — Systems must be robust, secure, and environmentally conscious.
6. **Accountability** — Clear chains of responsibility must be established for AI outcomes.
7. **Transparency** — AI systems and their decision-making processes must be explainable and auditable.

## Three-Tier Institutional Architecture

### 1. AI Governance Group (AIGG)
- **Function**: Inter-ministerial body for national AI policy coordination
- **Composition**: Representatives from MeitY, NITI Aayog, line ministries, and regulators
- **Mandate**: Develop guidelines, coordinate cross-sectoral AI strategy, monitor outcomes

### 2. Technology & Policy Expert Committee (TPEC)
- **Function**: Technical and legal advisory body
- **Composition**: AI researchers, legal experts, industry practitioners, civil society
- **Mandate**: Assess emerging risks, provide technical inputs to AIGG, draft standards

### 3. AI Safety Institute (AISI)
- **Function**: Safety testing, risk evaluation, benchmarking
- **Mandate**: Develop testing protocols for foundation models, evaluate systemic risks, establish national safety benchmarks

## Regulatory Instruments (2025–2026)

### IT Rules Amendment (February 2026)
- **Synthetically Generated Information (SGI)**: First statutory definition in Indian law
- **Deepfake Takedown**: Reduced from 36 hours to 2–3 hours for unlawful content
- **Traceability**: Platforms must implement technical disclosure for AI-generated content
- **Proactive Detection**: Shift from reactive, complaint-based to proactive AI content moderation

### Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDP Act, 2023)
- Governs data privacy, consent, and algorithmic processing
- Foundation for AI accountability — especially in automated decision-making

### Sectoral Regulators
- **RBI FREE-AI Framework (August 2025)**: Responsible and Ethical Enablement of AI in financial services
- **SEBI**: AI compliance measures for algorithmic trading and regulated entities
- **Digital India Act (Anticipated)**: Comprehensive replacement of IT Act 2000; AI-specific provisions expected

### Proposed Legislation
- **Artificial Intelligence (Ethics and Accountability) Bill, 2025**: Would establish statutory committees for algorithmic bias and surveillance oversight

## India's Governance Philosophy

### "Strategic Balancer" Approach
- **Light-touch**: Avoids EU-style prescriptive binding regulation
- **Pro-innovation**: Explicitly prioritizes innovation and economic growth
- **Risk-based**: Proportional regulatory intensity based on deployment context
- **Evolutionary**: "Voluntary today, mandatory tomorrow" — designed to tighten over time
- **Sectoral**: Leverages existing domain regulators (RBI, SEBI, TRAI) rather than creating a single AI regulator
- **DPI-Integrated**: AI governance layered atop India Stack (Aadhaar, UPI, DigiLocker)

### Key Tensions
1. Innovation enablement vs. citizen protection
2. Voluntary compliance vs. enforcement credibility
3. Global interoperability vs. sovereign governance
4. Speed of AI deployment vs. risk assessment capacity

## International Comparative Positioning

| Dimension | India | EU | US | China |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Framework Type** | Principle-based, voluntary | Binding regulation (EU AI Act) | Sector-specific, market-driven | State-directed, mandatory registration |
| **Risk Approach** | Risk-based, proportional | Risk-tiered (prohibited, high, limited, minimal) | Sector guidelines | Content + algorithmic alignment controls |
| **Institutional Design** | Multi-body (AIGG, TPEC, AISI) | EU AI Office + national authorities | NIST AI RMF + sector agencies | CAC + central registration |
| **Innovation Posture** | Pro-innovation | Precautionary | Market-permissive | State-channeled |
| **Deepfake Rules** | SGI labeling, fast takedowns | AI Act transparency obligations | Section 230 + state laws | Mandatory watermarking, CAC approval |
